## Direction contract

THESIS: The site is a small gallery hang of the studio's work, not a portfolio grid. Each piece is hung the way its medium lives: a report as an open spread, a campaign as a run of ads on one centre line, a book as an object, a site as a working screen. It refuses the uniform thumbnail grid and the centred template hero.

OWN-WORLD: Two painted gallery walls: Urbane Bronze (#54504A) and pale stone (#DDE2E1). Light pieces hang on bronze, dark pieces on stone; no colour accent, the primary action is a solid paper (#F2F0EB) button with bronze text. Besley (a Clarendon, exhibition signage) sets the H1 and section H2s; Schibsted Grotesk sets everything else. Museum wall labels (name, medium, one sentence) caption every piece, always under it, and the name links the whole piece to its case study. Pieces are flat and sharp-edged, share centre lines or baselines, never top-align by default. No frames, no shadows, no texture.

STORY: The visitor walks in, reads the exhibition title, sees the Pendulum spread at full scale, walks through rooms of work, reaches the information desk (four services), then the contact form.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Bronze wall. Besley title across the top in two lines. Below it, the Pendulum spread hung from column 5 and running off the right edge at a scale where the data reads. Intro, paper button, See the work, and the client credit line at 18px stack in columns 1 to 4 beside it.
